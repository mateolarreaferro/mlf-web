This pilot study investigates how combining auditory, visual, and haptic cues influences implicit learning and short-term memory retention. Participants completed an incidental categorization task followed by immediate and delayed recognition tests under either a visual-only or a trimodal condition. Trimodal stimulation produced consistently higher recognition accuracy both immediately and after a twenty-minute delay, with modest gains in response speed. The sample was small, but the findings suggest that haptic feedback alongside audiovisual stimuli may strengthen implicit memory traces and slow their decay, a proof of concept for real-time trimodal learning environments.
