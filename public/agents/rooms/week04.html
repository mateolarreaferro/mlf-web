week 4 · environment

Ludwig: a room to listen in

An agent with four microphones and no eyes stands among a few humming machines. It can wait, turn, walk, or say what it heard: how many machines, where, and which one just changed.

This week I built the environment my listening research needs. Ludwig is the perception half of what I am building at Attractor: SATIE generates acoustic worlds, Ludwig has to estimate what is in them from what a microphone array hears, over time, through silence and through its own motion. Until this week Ludwig could only replay recordings: it had an Action contract (wait, rotate, translate) that was validated but never executed. Now there is a world that executes it, a task distribution, a grader, a language-model listener and three scripted ones to compare it against.

Prior environments: SoundSpaces and SoundSpaces 2.0

I read Chen et al., "SoundSpaces: Audio-Visual Navigation in 3D Environments" (ECCV 2020) and "SoundSpaces 2.0: A Simulation Platform for Visual-Acoustic Learning" (NeurIPS 2022). They are the closest thing to an agent environment for hearing, and the second one validates itself in a way I wanted to copy.

State. A scanned scene (85 Matterport3D and 18 Replica scenes), the agent's node on a grid ("spatial resolution of 0.5m (Replica) or 1m (Matterport)", at 1.5 m height) and its heading, a fixed goal node and the sound playing there. There is no human in the world, so no cognitive state. The acoustics are precomputed: every source-listener pair's room impulse response ("producing N^2 RIRs" per scene) comes from geometric acoustics, "based on a bidirectional path tracing algorithm", with materials mapped from the mesh's semantic labels. SoundSpaces 2.0 makes the pose continuous and renders the response on the fly, with reflection, transmission and diffraction, "29 built-in acoustic materials", and mono, binaural, ambisonic or custom microphones.

Observation. One second of binaural audio as a log spectrogram ("65 x 65 x 2 and a 65 x 26 x 2 tensor" after downsampling), RGB and depth at 128x128 with a 90 degree field of view, and for the PointGoal tasks a displacement vector from "an idealized GPS sensor". The AudioGoal agent "does not receive a displacement vector". Hidden: the goal's position (it "has no visual presence"), the map, the materials.

Actions. "MoveForward, TurnLeft, TurnRight, and Stop"; a forward step is 0.5 m in Replica and 1 m in Matterport3D, and an invalid step "does nothing". The 2.0 continuous benchmark steps 0.15 m and turns 10 degrees.

Transition dynamics. Deterministic: "There is no actuation noise, i.e., a step executes perfectly or does not execute at all." The source is static and the scene never changes, so the only thing that moves is the agent. Nothing happens unless the agent acts. All the randomness is in episode generation.

Task generation. An episode is a scene, a start pose, a goal and a waveform, drawn by "choosing a scene and a random start and goal location", pruned when the geodesic distance is under 4 m or the ratio of geodesic to Euclidean distance is under 1.1, and the sound must be audible at the start. 0.1M (Replica) and 2M (Matterport3D) training episodes, 1000 test episodes per dataset, a "time horizon of 500 actions". 102 sounds, "73/11/18 splits for train/val/test", so unheard sounds and unseen scenes are both tested.

Russell and Norvig. Partially observable (the goal and the map are hidden; audio gives direction and distance only through the room). Single agent. Deterministic. Sequential (the policy has a GRU memory and the path matters). Static: the world waits. Discrete actions and grid states in 1.0, continuous pose in 2.0, continuous observations in both. Known and written down on the environment's side, unknown to the agent.

Evaluation. The unit is the episode. Success is "the agent executes the Stop action while being exactly at the location of the goal" (within 1 m in 2.0), and the headline number is SPL, "success rate normalized by inverse path length". Training uses a shaped reward: "+10 for executing Stop at the goal location, a negative reward of -0.01 per time step, +1 for reducing the geodesic distance to the goal, and the equivalent penalty for increasing it". Numbers are averaged over 1000 episodes and 7 runs.

Validation. The 2020 paper validates nothing against the world: "The fidelity of the sound renderings can be experienced in our project page videos". The 2022 paper measures real impulse responses in a real apartment whose mesh is in Replica, at "seven different source/receiver positions", and compares direct-to-reverberant ratio and reverberation time: the DRR error is "reduced from 11.0 dB to 0.98 dB on average, while preserving the same relative RT60 error of 12.4%". A speech recogniser trained on its rooms reaches 12.48% word error on real reverberant test data, against 18.48% for 1.0 and 13.32% when trained on real responses. A footnote admits the mesh materials "were optimized to match the measurements".

What I took. Borrow: the seeded task distribution with constraints that keep episodes solvable but not trivial; the episode as the unit with success and efficiency reported separately; the habit of validating against a real measurement with a number and a caveat. Change: my world should not wait for the agent, the sensor should be the array format my real recordings use rather than binaural spectrograms, and the agent should be judged on what it says is there, not on reaching a place. Avoid: an idealized sensor with no noise or delay, agents trained for hundreds of GPU hours on one world, and realism asserted from videos.

The task environment

Why an environment. My claim is about state over time: how many sounding things there are, where, which one changed, after silence, after one's own motion. A text answer to "what do you hear in this clip" cannot test that, because the clip is fixed and nothing the agent does changes what comes next. Here the agent decides what it will hear. Waiting reveals an event. Walking separates two machines that share a bearing, and costs the tracker its memory. Turning tests whether beliefs survive the agent's own motion. Acting has consequences and a budget.

Cognitive augmentation. The agent keeps a listening watch so a person does not have to: a workshop, a plant room, a studio, someone whose hearing is impaired, or a sound designer checking that the machines in a scene can be told apart. The human capacity it stands in for is sustained auditory attention and memory over minutes, which people are bad at.

Objectives. Count the machines and locate them; notice which one changed and say what happened (accelerated, slowed, stopped, started, moved); do it with the least time and walking; stay honest when two cannot be told apart. The grader reports these separately. The environment carries no reward.

Success and failure in the real problem. Success is a report a person could act on: "three machines, the one on the left just stopped." Failure is a missed stop (the one that matters), a phantom machine (which erodes trust in every later report), a wrong bearing, or an answer that arrives after the budget.

ComponentThis environment
Environment state (S)Sample clock; listener position and yaw; 1 to 3 rotating machines, each with position, velocity, shaft rate f0 (180 to 420 Hz), eight harmonic amplitudes, phase, operating mode (steady, accelerating, decelerating, stationary), emission on or off, and a rate factor; the isotropic noise floor; the event schedule (one event, its machine, its time); remaining listening, rotation and walking budgets; pending action feedback. No human in the world.
Observation space (O)One record per 100 ms: four-channel first-order Ambisonic audio (ACN W,Y,Z,X, SN3D, 24 kHz, PCM16), available 20 ms after capture; the measured pose with 1 mm and 0.001 rad noise, available 15 ms after measurement; feedback for completed or rejected actions. Nothing else. Hidden: positions and ranges, the count, modes, emission, identities, the noise level, the future. Proxies: a direction vote is not a machine (two on one bearing are one vote), a pitch estimate is not a rotor rate (octave errors near the band edge; in a mix the stronger machine wins), a silence is not a stop (it may be masking).
Action space (A)Ludwig's Action contract: wait(duration); rotate_sensor(yaw, duration) up to 1 rad/s; translate_sensor(dx, dy in the sensor frame, duration) up to 0.5 m/s on the floor. Durations in multiples of 100 ms. Over budget, over speed or off the floor: rejected, no time passes, the rejection rides on the next observation. The report that ends an episode (count, azimuths, the event and where) is the answer, written also as a final BeliefState record.
Transition dynamicsEach 100 ms: machines follow their schedule (mode, rate, emission, motion); the listener moves by its share of the running action; each emitting machine is a sum of eight harmonics of f0 times its rate factor, with continuous phase, amplitude 0.03/r, encoded as a plane wave from its sensor-frame direction; a machine running down loses level with its rate; isotropic Gaussian noise at the task's level. One event per episode at a seeded time between 6 s and 60% of the budget: none; accelerate (rate to 1.4 over 5 s); decelerate (to 0.7 over 5 s); stop (to 0.5 over 3 s, then silent for good); start (a silent machine runs up over 3 s); move (0.35 m/s sideways for 8 s). Events happen whether or not the agent acts. Not modelled: propagation delay, Doppler, room response, occlusion, measured sensor response, motion within a chunk.
Initial state / task distributionAll from the seed: machine count (1 to 3), azimuths at least 35 degrees apart unless an aligned pair is drawn (a quarter of episodes: two machines within 2 to 8 degrees of one bearing, 1.5 to 2.5 m apart in range), ranges 1.5 to 5 m, elevations within 10 degrees, shaft rates at least 25 Hz apart, harmonic amplitudes, the event kind, which machine and when. The listener starts at the origin facing forward. Budgets: 30 s of listening, two turns, 4 m of walking. Same seed, same episode.
Agent objective / rewardNone inside the environment. The grader reports count correct, bearings matched at 20 degrees against the final truth, event kind correct and located, and costs (seconds, metres, radians, model calls and tokens), plus a passive bearing diagnostic over the per-chunk tracker beliefs.
Stopping conditionThe agent reports, or the listening budget runs out (then it is scored as having reported nothing).

Russell and Norvig. Partially observable: everything that matters is inferred from four audio channels and a noisy, late pose. Single agent: the machines are scheduled, not agents. Stochastic from the agent's side (the noise is drawn every chunk, the pose measurement is noisy); deterministic given the seed. Sequential: what was heard before the event is what makes the event legible, and a walk changes every later observation. Dynamic: the world moves while the agent thinks and the budget runs during a wait. Continuous state, time in 100 ms steps, continuous action parameters on a discrete set of kinds. Known to the environment and written down in one file; unknown to the agent, which gets the rules in its prompt but not the numbers.

How it is built

Explicit state lives in Environment and Machine in environment.py. reset() draws a task instance from the seed and returns the first second of observations; step(action) validates the record against the contract and the budget, executes it over its duration, renders each chunk, writes a truth record per chunk, and returns the observations heard. The interface is the four versioned JSON records Ludwig already had (Observation, GroundTruthState, BeliefState, Action) with their validators. The agents import nothing from the environment but the task type, the environment class and three constants; the environment imports nothing from the agents; the grader verifies the run's seal before it opens the truth. Different seeds give different instances and the same seed gives the same one, both tested.

Every agent shares one perception module: the DSP bearing tracker I run on real recordings, wrapped to keep a history and to attach a pitch estimate and a level per direction. On top of it sit the policies. The language-model listener is Claude Opus 5.5 with four tools (wait, rotate, translate, report), reading the history as one line per second. The three scripted listeners make no choices: stand still; turn a full circle; stand, step one metre left, stand; and they report with fixed heuristics over the same history. The whole exchange of the model is saved per episode.

An example run

The model waited 5 s, 8 s and 4 s, then reported at 18 s: two machines, one "loud machine ~290 Hz" at -116°, now stationary, one "quieter machine ~358 Hz" at -122°, event: stop at -116°. Its note: "The 290 Hz source at -116° dropped in pitch (to about 240 Hz) and fell silent around t=12-14 s. Once it was gone, a steady, quieter 358 Hz machine showed up on almost the same bearing (-122°), where the louder one had been masking it." Count, both bearings within 2°, and the event right; four model calls, 10,089 tokens in and 640 out; 18 of 30 s used, no walking. The three scripted listeners called the same episode an acceleration, because the slowing fundamental fell below the tracker's 200 Hz band and the pitch estimate doubled.

Validation

What matters most for my claim is that the observation really carries the spatial and spectral information a listener would get from such a scene, and that the difficulty is in the right place. Three checks, about half an hour of compute and reading.

  1. Convention (self-consistency). The localizer that matched real STARSS23 recordings in my earlier pilot reads the environment's Ambisonics at the azimuths the environment states: eight single-machine seeds, maximum error 0.005°. This says the channel order, normalization and sign conventions agree with the real format. It says nothing about realism.
  2. Inverse-distance law (theory grounding). Walking toward a machine raises its received level by 20 log10(r0/r1), exactly, on six seeds. It is the law the renderer implements. Real rooms add a reverberant floor that flattens it; a known gap.
  3. Real-data alignment. The passive tracker, standing still, on eight seeded episodes of 20 s at each of eight noise levels, against my frozen four-clip STARSS23 pilot (precision 35.4%, recall 54.4%, matched error 12.0° at a 20° gate).

Recall can be brought to the real pilot's level by raising the noise floor. Precision and angular error cannot: the environment has no sounds that are not machines, so almost every direction it emits matches a true one, and plane waves give directions a degree wide. In real rooms wrong directions come from reflections and interference, not from a noise floor. The 10% of misses at every level are the environment's own hard cases: aligned pairs merged into one cluster, two machines passing through one pitch, decelerations below the band edge. What stays uncertain: whether the harmonic hum and the five events are what real rotating machines sound like when they change state (that is the instrumented pilot with real motors in Ludwig's plan, not this environment), and whether a listener that does well here does well in a room. The matching says the environment is easier in exactly the way a free field is.

Failure analysis

  1. Pitch in a mixed cluster follows the stronger machine (seed 106, model and scripts both wrong). Two machines eight degrees apart are one cluster for the tracker. The quieter one accelerated toward the louder one's harmonics and the cluster's pitch estimate jumped from 334 to about 300 Hz. The model read a stop (it even noted that "none of the single-machine events fits"); the heuristics read nothing. The weak part is the percept, not the world: a per-source pitch needs the harmonics of one machine grouped by direction consistency, which the tracker does not do.
  2. Walking costs identity (seed 111, model wrong on the count; the still listener was right). A translation of more than 2 cm resets the tracker, so each of the model's two walks threw its tracks away, and the quieter of two machines 50° apart never accumulated enough support to be counted. The dynamics are right here, that is the real trade-off, but the reset rule is crude because the tracker has no range. The picture of that episode.
  3. An aligned pair is resolved only by luck or by walking (seed 103). Nobody separated two machines on one bearing until one of them stopped. The model did not try walking there, and the fixed 1 m sidestep did not separate them either.
  4. Decelerations fall off the band. A stop runs the rotor to half speed; a fundamental near 200 Hz drops below the tracker's lower edge and the pitch sieve reports an octave up, so the scripts called three stops accelerations.
  5. The sidestep script confuses move with start on three-machine seeds: after its walk the moving machine has a new id and a new bearing and looks like a newcomer.
  6. Rotation is pointless here. In a free field with an isotropic array and a compensated pose, turning changes nothing the agent can use, and the model never turned. An environment in which rotation earns information needs a directional sensor or a non-isotropic noise field.

Unvalidated, and where it should become more realistic: the machine sound and the five events are chosen, not measured. There is no room, so precision and angular error cannot be matched to real recordings without reflections and sounds that are not machines. Agents run in the same process as the environment, so the separation is by interface, not by an operating-system boundary. The grader scores bearings at the final pose only, so a report that was right before the last walk and wrong after it gets no credit. Next: a single reflection, one clutter source that is not a machine, a tracker that keeps its tracks through a walk, and real motor recordings in place of the hum.

Reflection

The thing I did not expect is how little the policy mattered. I spent the week on the environment and the interface, and when the four listeners finally ran on the same seeds, standing still was right nine times out of twelve. The ears carry the difficulty. The model earned its two extra episodes by reading the history well (it understood that a stop had uncovered a second machine) and by stopping early, not by moving cleverly. When it did move, it sometimes lost more than it gained. That is exactly the trade-off I wanted the environment to expose, and it exposed it on me.

The second thing is what the contracts did for me. Because the only way in and out of the environment is four JSON records that were already defined, I could not cheat: there was no way to hand the agent a position, and the grader could not read the truth until the run was sealed. The environment's observation package is an ordinary recorded episode, so the passive replay I had before runs on what the agent heard. The interface made the separation real instead of a diagram.

The third thing is that validating against real data was humbling in a useful way. I could match recall with a noise knob and nothing else. The environment is easy in exactly the way a free field is easy, and the number says so. What I would change first is the room: one reflection and one sound that is not a machine would move precision and angular error toward the real pilot's, and would make turning worth something.
